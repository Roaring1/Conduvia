    Tier 0 — Survival¶

    Stone, sticks, and the next few hours. Solve water, fire, food, and sleep before they solve you.

    0
    Tier 0 — Survival
    Unlocks after: You wake up with nothing.→Leads to: Fiber, fire, water, a cutting edge — a night you survive.

    14 quests · 6 on the win-path · 7 branches

    Briefing

    You Woke Up Here¶

    Conduvia¶

    You have nothing. What you make of that is up to you.

    This first place is Earth: trees, stone, water, clay, ore, and all the awkward steps between raw material and working technology.

    Do now:

    1. Grab useful debris with your Interact key.
    2. Turn plant fiber into your first useful material.
    3. Mark a camp so production has a home.

    The early survival layer is background pressure, not the point. The point is the chain: hand tools → materials → heat → metals → power → machines → automation → worlds beyond this one.

    Tip · Need controls or deeper details? The section below has the full field guide. You can also open Settings -> Controls to rebind anything.

    Background

    Survival priorities¶

    Four things will get you if you ignore them:

    1. Water
    2. Fire
    3. Food
    4. Sleep

    In that order, roughly. Try not to lose track of the first one while solving the fourth.

    How to gather things¶

    Most materials come from walking up to something and using your Interact key.

    • Plant fibers -- Interact with tall grass, ferns, or leafy shrubs
    • River stones -- Interact with rocks near water or rocky ground
    • Bark slabs / bark strips -- Interact with tree trunks (aim at the bark, not the canopy)
    • Twigs / sticks -- Interact with fallen branches and forest floor debris
    • Berries / nuts -- Interact with shrubs and tree drip lines
    • Dry logs -- Interact with dead snags (the bare-trunk dead trees)

    If nothing happens when you use Interact: try crouching (Left Ctrl) or getting closer.

    Tip · Hover anything in your inventory and use the Uses key (U by default) to see every recipe that uses it. The Recipes key (R by default) goes the other way — "what recipes make this?"

    Quest book¶

    The left rail is the chapter list — each chapter is one tier of progression. Nodes inside a chapter are quests; lines between them are prerequisites. Click any node to see what it needs and what it rewards.

    Bookmarks (left rail of the crafting pane) let you pin favorite items and recipes you're actively tracking.

    Crafting + items¶

    Open inventory with the inventory key (E by default). Hover any item and use Recipes (R by default) to see recipes that produce it, or Uses (U by default) to see what it becomes. Use Favorite (A by default) to pin items you are tracking. Some recipes need a station — the quest will say which one. Put a placeable station on your hotbar/equipped slot to enter placement mode.

    When a machine just sits there¶

    Later, machines are not supposed to be mysterious. If one is idle, blocked, or paused, read it like a mechanic with a clipboard:

    1. Open the station panel — the bottom status area tells you missing inputs, output-full, or power/feed problems.
    2. Point at the station or pipe with the Inspector — it reports live power, flow, pressure, source/sink counts, and filter problems.
    3. Check the recipe card — red shortage chips tell you the exact missing ingredients.
    4. Check the map after surveying — identified nodes add waypoints so you can actually find your feedstock again.

    No random breakdowns are hiding in the bushes to trick you. If the route has input, output space, the right station, and compatible power, it will work. If it doesn't, check your diagnostic readouts. The game isn't hiding the problem from you; you just have to look at the gauges.

    Death + respawn¶

    Dying leaves a gravestone at the death site holding your inventory. A waypoint auto-marks it on the map so you can walk back. The grave doesn't expire and is open to anyone nearby.

    Craft a bedroll, place it, then use its Set Home prompt to claim a spawn point. You'll respawn there instead of the default pad.

    What kind of game is this?¶

    Conduvia is a slow-burn technology game about turning raw materials into working systems. You start with stone, sticks, clay, water, and ore. Later, the same chain reaches machines, automation, aerospace, and worlds beyond this first one.

    Every step tries to respect how the thing actually works. Charcoal takes a clamp kiln and real burn time. Iron needs a bloomery and a hammer. You use the best process available for what you have.

    The start is small on purpose. The payoff is when food, fuel, parts, and power stop being chores because the system you built is doing them without you.

    Win-path

    Cordage (The First Multiplier)¶

    Unlocks after: You Woke Up Here

    Cordage¶

    Cordage is the first small material chain: loose fibers become something structural.

    Get your first length of rope. One is enough to learn the loop; later builds will ask for cordage when the structure actually needs tying, binding, or lashing.

    Tip · Plant fibers are found on the ground. Look for tall ferns or leafy shrubs and press Interact to pick them up. If you can't find any, you can also strip bark from a tree trunk, put 3 strips + 1 bark_bucket_water in the camp marker and craft plant_fibers.

    Tip · Rope itself is hand-crafted from 3 plant_fibers. Hover rope and use Recipes (R by default) to see it.

    Do this: - retrieve Goal: end up with at least 1 rope in your inventory (craft it, or pick it up — either works).
    yields: rope×1 - craft Suggested way to get it: craft rope from 3 plant fibers. (Optional — skip if you already found rope.) optional
    needs: plant fibers×3
    yields: rope×1

    Background

    Cordage isn't flashy — it just keeps showing up.¶

    The three obtain paths for plant fibers (fern, willow, bark-retting) mean you're never truly stuck for cordage as long as you have a forest to work with.

    Win-path

    Get a Starter Pile + Mark a Spot¶

    Unlocks after: Cordage (The First Multiplier)

    Mark your first campsite¶

    Your next real goal is a camp marker. It turns the forest from "random stuff on the ground" into a place where early station recipes make sense.

    You only need 8 twigs, 2 stones, and 1 rope for the marker. The bigger starter pile is optional padding, not a grind wall.

    Do this: - retrieve Optional: gather a starter pile so you do fewer emergency trips later. optional
    yields: fine forest twigs×10 rounded river stone×4 plant fibers×6 (or fieldstone×4) - craft Twist a quick rope tie for the camp marker. optional
    needs: plant fibers×4
    yields: rope×1 - craft Craft a simple camp marker. optional
    needs: fine forest twigs×8 rounded river stone×2 rope×1
    yields: camp marker (simple)×1 - retrieve Place the camp marker: put it on your hotbar, equip it, aim the green ghost at dry ground, then LMB to place. R rotates; Esc cancels.
    yields: camp marker (simple)×1

    Background

    Place first, stockpile second¶

    Make the marker, put it somewhere dry and flat, then use it as your first work anchor. Extra twigs, stone, and fibers are useful, but they are not the main gate.

    Win-path

    Get One Real Cutting Edge¶

    Unlocks after: Get a Starter Pile + Mark a Spot

    Get a cutting edge¶

    Your hands are terrible scissors. Fix that.

    A stone flake is the first tool that earns its name — something that actually cuts on purpose. Without it, you're just pulling things apart. With it, you're manufacturing.

    Do this: - retrieve Have a sharp stone flake.
    yields: stone flake×1 - craft Craft stone flakes at the camp marker: 1 rounded stone + 1 basic hammerstone tool. optional
    needs: rounded river stone×1 hammerstone (hafted)×1
    yields: stone flake×1

    Background

    Working Smart¶

    A flake is the sharp piece you get when you strike stone against stone the right way. Make a hammerstone from rounded stones, turn it into a basic hammerstone tool, then use that tool at the camp marker to knock flakes from another rounded stone.

    Hunting (Or: Gathering Leather)¶

    A stone flake is also your first hunting tool. You aren't doing this for sport; you are doing it because animals are walking leather repositories.

    1. Equip the stone flake from your hotbar.
    2. Walk up to an animal and left-click to strike.
    3. Pick up the carcass and take it to a butchering block. Break it down into meat, hide, and bones.

    Start with passive animals: Rabbits and deer don't bite back. Wolves do. Don't fight a wolf with a rock.

    Tip · The hide you get from butchering is how you make rawhide_cord -- absolutely essential for better tools.

    Branch

    Solve Water (For Now)¶

    Unlocks after: Cordage (The First Multiplier), Get One Real Cutting Edge

    Solve water (for now)¶

    Find a water source, bring it back, make it safe.

    The real problem isn't finding water — it's the ten round trips after your camp is set up somewhere else. Make a bark bucket. Carrying water in cupped hands is technically possible, but it's terrible engineering.

    Do this: - retrieve Gather bark strips and a dry stick (for the bucket handle). optional
    yields: curled bark strip×3 small dry sticks×1 - craft Craft a bark bucket (3 bark strips + 2 plant fibers + 1 stick, at camp marker). optional
    needs: curled bark strip×3 plant fibers×2 small dry sticks×1
    yields: bark bucket×1 - retrieve Bring water back to your camp.
    yields: water-filled bark bucket×1 - craft Treat the water so it's safe to drink.
    needs: water-filled bark bucket×1
    yields: treated water (bark bucket)×1

    Background

    Logistics 101¶

    Early camps don't fail because there's no water. They fail because water is a long walk, five times a day. Solve the transport problem early.

    Boiling guidance¶

    Water from a river will make you sick. Boil it. If your container can't sit in a flame (like a bark bucket), use hot-stone boiling.

    Win-path

    Make Fire Repeatable¶

    Unlocks after: Get One Real Cutting Edge, Cordage (The First Multiplier)

    Make fire repeatable¶

    Getting fire isn't the hard part. Keeping it alive is.

    Three-layer stack — each one depends on the last: - Tinder -- catches the spark (dry fiber tinder) - Kindling -- builds it into a flame (dry kindling sticks) - Fuel -- keeps it going long enough to matter (small dry sticks or logs)

    To light the campfire: equip the fire_drill_kit, walk up to a placed campfire, and left-click (LMB). The kit consumes dry tinder, the fire catches, and the kit wears with use — craft a spare.

    Tip · The fire ring (6 river stones at camp marker) is optional — a campfire without a ring still works, it just doesn't look intentional.

    Do this: - retrieve Have a fire drill kit in your inventory.
    yields: fire drill kit×1 - place Place a survival campfire (crafted at the camp marker).
    yields: survival campfire×1 - action Light the placed campfire: equip the fire drill kit, stand close, and press LMB. Needs 1 dry fiber tinder. - retrieve Optional: build a stone fire ring first (6 river stones at camp marker). optional
    yields: stone fire ring×1 - retrieve Optional: stock tinder and kindling before building. optional
    yields: dry fiber tinder×3 dry kindling sticks×8 - retrieve Optional: stock dry fuel sticks. optional
    yields: small dry sticks×6 - craft Craft a basic fire drill kit (camp marker: 2 dry sticks + rope + stone flake). optional
    needs: small dry sticks×2 rope×1 stone flake×1
    yields: fire drill kit×1 - craft Build a stone fire ring (camp marker: 6 river stones). optional
    needs: rounded river stone×6
    yields: stone fire ring×1 - craft Build a survival campfire (camp marker: fire ring + tinder + kindling + logs). optional
    needs: stone fire ring×1 dry fiber tinder×2 dry kindling sticks×4 small dry log×2
    yields: survival campfire×1

    Background

    Think like an engineer¶

    You don't want a bonfire. You want a small fire, a steady feed, and control over when it dies. The fire_drill_kit is a reliable ignition tool. Equip it, stand near the campfire, and press LMB. It spins, consumes your tinder, and creates heat.

    Win-path

    Set Up a Night That Doesn't Wipe You¶

    Unlocks after: Make Fire Repeatable

    Set up a night that doesn't wreck you¶

    The ground steals your body heat. Put something between you and it.

    Make a straw bedroll, find somewhere reasonably sheltered, and put it down.

    Tip · To sleep: place the bedroll, walk up to it, and use the sleep prompt (E by default), or open the bedroll panel and press Sleep. Resting restores stamina and costs a little nutrition; the world clock keeps moving, so treat it as rest, not a magic sun button.

    Tip · Hover bedroll_straw and use Recipes (R by default) to see the crafting recipe (2x thatch bundle + plant fibres, at a camp marker).

    Do this: - retrieve Have or place a straw bedroll.
    yields: straw bedroll×1 - craft Craft a straw bedroll (2x thatch bundle + plant fibres, at camp marker). optional
    needs: bundle of thatch×2 plant fibers×1
    yields: straw bedroll×1 - place Place your bedroll somewhere sheltered, press E or the Sleep button to rest, and use Set Home if you want it as your respawn point. optional
    yields: straw bedroll×1

    Background

    Bedding is layered¶

    Grass, leaves, dry boughs — piled thick. If the ground's cold, you'll feel it through almost anything thin.

    Wind and wet can drop your temperature even above freezing. A windbreak and a roof matter as much as the bedding itself.

    A Quieter Hunt¶

    Unlocks after: Cordage (The First Multiplier)

    A Quieter Hunt¶

    Not everything is worth killing.

    A lasso and a basket let you move small animals around — relocate prey closer to camp, or just keep a few around for the mid-game when you need steady yields without burning through your hunting stock.

    How it works:

    1. Craft a rope lasso (2 rope + 1 rawhide cord) at the hand-crafting grid.
    2. Craft a wicker basket (2 willow + 1 plant fibers).
    3. Equip the lasso. Walk within about 15 studs of a Rabbit, Bird, or Owl and left-click.
    4. The animal is packed into a basket in your inventory.
    5. Equip the occupied basket and left-click to release the animal wherever you want it.

    Getting rawhide cord: To craft the lasso you need rawhide_cord. Kill a passive animal (Rabbit, Deer, Cow) by equipping your stone flake and left-clicking it. Pick up the carcass with Interact, take it to a butchering block, and use Interact to get hide. Then hand-craft: hide + stone_flake -> rawhide_cord.

    Alternative — snares: Rather than chasing animals, set a passive snare. Craft a snare_simple (rope + 2 forest_twigs_fine, hand grid) and drop it near an animal trail. Small game will walk into it and you'll find a carcass when you come back.

    Tip · Rope lasso is single-use — it's consumed on a successful catch. Bring a few if you plan to move a group.

    Tip · For medium animals (Cow, Sheep, Deer, Red Fox) you need a wooden cage (2 wood + 1 rope + 1 rawhide cord). Same mechanic.

    Tip · Wolves and bears can't be lassooed. Craft a wolf lure (cooked meat + rope) or bear lure (2 safe berries + rope) instead — place it and step back. The animal arrives on its own, then you deal with it however you like.

    Do this: - craft Craft a rope lasso.
    needs: rope×2 rawhide cord×1
    yields: rope lasso×1 - craft Craft a wicker basket (2 willow wands + 1 plant fibers, hand-crafted).
    yields: wicker basket×1 - retrieve Catch a small animal (have any occupied basket).
    yields: wicker basket (Rabbit)×1 wicker basket (Bird)×1 wicker basket (Owl)×1 - craft Optional: also set a snare for passive trapping (rope + 2 twigs, hand grid). optional
    needs: rope×1 fine forest twigs×2
    yields: simple snare×1

    Rewards: rope×2 rawhide cord×1

    Background

    Why bother?¶

    Lasso-and-basket is a soft version of ranching. You're not taming the animals — they're still wild when you release them. But being able to relocate them means you can consolidate livestock near camp, seed a pond with birds, or just move a cow somewhere convenient before the mid-game farms come online.

    The wooden cage opens up deer relocation, which feeds into leather supply chains before you get consistent hunting going.

    Snare vs lasso¶

    Snares are for food. Lassos are for relocation. Both require rawhide cord, but snares are lighter to set up and let you trap passively while you do other things. A few snares set before bed means meat in the morning without a hunt.

    First Cast¶

    Unlocks after: Get One Real Cutting Edge

    First Cast¶

    Still water holds food.

    Craft a basic fishing rod at your camp marker and try your luck near a river or pond. It takes patience — the float bobs, then dips. Press your Interact key when it dips.

    How it works:

    1. Craft a fishing rod at the camp marker (2 dry sticks + 3 plant fibers + 1 stone flake).
    2. Stand within 14 studs of water. Right-click to cast.
    3. Wait for the float to dip — then press your Interact key to reel in.

    Tip · The bite window is short (~1.8 s). Watch the timing bar and don't wait too long.

    Tip · Missed the window? Right-click to cast again. Rod has limited durability — around 20 catches.

    Do this: - craft Craft a basic fishing rod.
    yields: basic fishing rod×1 - retrieve Catch a raw fish.
    yields: raw fish×1

    Rewards: small dry sticks×2

    Background

    Fishing is patience, not luck.¶

    The float dips when the fish strikes. React within the window or the fish spits the hook.

    A good riverside camp near the water means you can fish between other tasks without burning much time.

    Get Calories In¶

    Unlocks after: Make Fire Repeatable

    Get some calories in¶

    Go find food. Cook some of it.

    Different shrubs and trees drop different fruits and nuts — blueberry, blackberry, hawthorn, sea buckthorn, juniper, rowan; hazelnut, acorn, nut bundles. Any pair of the same kind sorts at hand into one safe-edible portion (R-key the berry to see the recipe). Cooked food beats raw food.

    Do this: - retrieve Gather a small amount of safe foods.
    yields: safe berries×8 safe nuts×4 - retrieve Have at least one simple cooked ration ready.
    yields: simple cooked food×1 - produce Cook one simple ration at your campfire. optional

    Rewards: simple cooked food×1

    Background

    Don't eat it all immediately¶

    If you're always running at zero food, every other task gets harder.

    Cook a small batch, eat one, keep the rest. Running a surplus sounds boring — it isn't, once you see what it unlocks.

    Tip · The world has an assortment of fruits and nuts, not just one generic kind. Pick whichever your area happens to grow — they all funnel into the same food_berries_safe / food_nuts_safe output.

    Dirt Under Your Nails¶

    Unlocks after: Get Calories In

    Dirt Under Your Nails¶

    Foraging works today. Next week it won't.

    Gathering seeds now means you hit Tier 0.5 with something ready to plant.

    Getting seeds:

    Flowers and spent grass heads drop forest_seed_mix_dry when you use Interact. Take 3 handfuls to your camp marker and sort them into crop seeds (recipe: "sort seed mix" in the camp marker's crafting list). This gives flax, turnip, AND carrot seeds in one pass.

    The full farming loop (needs hoe, available in Tier 0.5):

    1. Equip a hoe and swing it at open ground (grass or dirt) to till a patch.
    2. Walk up to the tilled soil with seeds in your inventory and use Interact to plant.
    3. Wait for the sprout to change colour — it's ripe when it glows bright.
    4. Use Interact to harvest: you get crop + a seed back, so the supply sustains itself.

    Tip · Turnip is the staple starter crop — fast and hardy. Carrot is a bit slower but useful for variety.

    Do this: - retrieve Have carrot seeds. Sort them at your camp marker (3 seed mix -> all three seed types) or via the hand grid (2 seed mix + plant fibers -> carrot seed).
    yields: carrot seed×2 - retrieve Also have some turnip seeds for the staple crop. optional
    yields: turnip seed×2

    Rewards: carrot seed×2 turnip seed×2

    Background

    Why bother with farming?¶

    Hunting and foraging are unreliable and biome-dependent. Farming gives you a controlled, renewable food loop that works regardless of how far you've explored.

    One tilled row of turnips takes less time per calorie than any hunting route.

    Starting the seed collection now means you hit Tier 0.5 with something to plant immediately rather than having to backtrack for seeds after you've built the hoe.

    Keep What You Find¶

    Unlocks after: Set Up a Night That Doesn't Wipe You

    Keep What You Find¶

    Your pack fills fast. A bark storage crate holds 27 stacks and keeps its contents between sessions — everything you place in it stays there when you log off.

    To craft one, open your camp marker and queue a bark storage crate: - 8 x forest bark strip - 2 x branch bundle - 2 x rope

    The camp marker shows a progress bar while it builds (~50 s). When done, place the crate near your bedroll.

    To use it, left-click the crate to open it. Shift+click moves items between your inventory and the crate instantly.

    Do this: - craft Queue 'bark storage crate' at the camp marker (8 bark strips + 2 branch bundles + 2 rope).
    needs: curled bark strip×8 branch bundle×2 rope×2
    yields: bark storage crate×1 - place Equip the bark storage crate and use Interact to place it near camp.
    yields: bark storage crate×1

    Rewards: curled bark strip×6 rope×2

    Make Camp Permanent¶

    Unlocks after: Keep What You Find

    Make Camp Permanent¶

    A bedroll and a crate make a spot. Stone, clay, hide, and thatch make a camp -- one that keeps the rain off, holds water, and survives you walking away from it.

    None of this needs a station. It is all hand-built from what you are already gathering, just in larger amounts. Prepare cordage and clay before you start — the roof and walls are hungry for both.

    What you are building:

    • Fire pit -- a ring of river stones that turns a campfire into a fixed hearth (8 river stones + 4 sticks).
    • Wattle-and-daub wall -- woven sticks packed with wet clay; the first real wall (8 sticks + 4 clay + 2 water).
    • Thatch roof -- bundled thatch lashed down with rope; sheds rain (8 thatch bundles + 4 rope).
    • Rawhide waterskin -- a stitched hide bag so you can carry water from the river (2 hide + 3 rawhide cord).
    • Coiled basket -- bark coiled and bound with cord; hauls loose forage and stone (6 bark strips + 2 rawhide cord).

    Tip · Queue each build at your camp marker, the same way you made the storage crate.

    Do this: - craft Queue 'fire pit' at the camp marker (8 river stones + 4 sticks).
    needs: rounded river stone×8 small dry sticks×4
    yields: fire pit×1 - craft Queue 'wattle and daub wall' at the camp marker (8 sticks + 4 clay + 2 water).
    needs: small dry sticks×8 raw clay×4 raw water×2
    yields: wattle-and-daub wall×1 - craft Queue 'thatch roof' at the camp marker (8 thatch bundles + 4 rope).
    needs: bundle of thatch×8 rope×4
    yields: thatched roof×1 - craft Queue 'rawhide waterskin' at the camp marker (2 hide + 3 rawhide cord).
    needs: raw animal hide×2 rawhide cord×3
    yields: rawhide waterskin×1 - craft Queue 'coiled basket' at the camp marker (6 bark strips + 2 rawhide cord).
    needs: curled bark strip×6 rawhide cord×2
    yields: basket (coiled)×1 - place Place the fire pit, wall, and roof to finish your permanent camp.
    yields: fire pit×1

    Rewards: rope×4 rawhide cord×4
