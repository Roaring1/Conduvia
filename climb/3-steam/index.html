    Tier 3 — Steam¶

    Mechanical power. Coke gives you furnace-grade heat; steam gives you work that never gets tired.

    3
    Tier 3 — Steam
    Unlocks after: Bronze and wrought iron tools.→Leads to: Coke, coal tar, mechanical power.

    11 quests · 9 on the win-path · 1 branches

    Briefing

    Coal, Waterpower, and Early Steam¶

    Tier 2 is where "site" starts to matter¶

    You are no longer just a camp with better tools. You are a works:

    • waterpower with line-shaft distribution
    • coal access (the fuel that breaks woodland limits)
    • coke production for ironworks-grade heat
    • a boiler house with a real steam loop
    • your first serious steam use is mine drainage (pumping), then expanded into rotary shop drive

    Tip · You need a bronze pickaxe or iron hammer (from the Metal Ages chapter) to mine coal and hematite. Make sure you have one before tackling coal access.

    Tip · New rule for powered industry: read the machine before blaming the recipe. Station panels and the Inspector now say things like Waiting for shaft/drive power, Output full, or Missing: 2x wrought iron plate. If a wheel, shaft, boiler, pipe, or mill is not behaving, inspect the actual placed part — the game is trying to tell you which real-world condition is missing.

    Background

    Keep this tier honest¶

    Steam starts as stationary power. Not trains. Not magic torque. Pumps first. Rotation second.

    Win-path

    Put a Stream to Work¶

    Unlocks after: Coal, Waterpower, and Early Steam

    Put a stream to work¶

    Build an overshot wheel where you have usable head and couple it to a short shaft.

    We aren't burning fuel yet because we don't have to. If gravity and water will do the heavy lifting for free, let them.

    Do this: - retrieve Have or place an overshot waterwheel.
    yields: waterwheel (overshot)×1 - retrieve Have a first short line shaft coupled to the wheel (basic mechanical output).
    yields: wrought-iron line shaft×1

    Rewards: wrought-iron line shaft×1

    Background

    What "good placement" means¶

    • you need fall (head), not just flow
    • you need a stable channel intake
    • you need a safe spot for the rotating assembly

    You could have built a wooden wheel centuries ago. You couldn't have built the iron shaft, stone millrace, and timber gearing that make it actually useful. Now you can.

    Win-path

    Line-Shaft Workshop Bay¶

    Unlocks after: Put a Stream to Work

    Line-shaft workshop bay¶

    Extend shafting and use belts + sheaves to distribute power to tools.

    This is the "factory floor" pattern: one prime mover -> many driven stations.

    Do this: - retrieve Have additional shafting and belt drive parts ready.
    yields: wrought-iron line shaft×2 leather flat belt×4 sheave×4 - retrieve Bring at least one driven tool online (air for fire or force for forming).
    yields: bellows×1 lever press×1

    Rewards: leather flat belt×2 sheave×2

    Background

    What this unlocks¶

    You stop asking: "can I power this tool?"

    You start asking: "how many tools can this shaft feed?"

    Win-path

    Open the Coal Measures¶

    Unlocks after: Coal, Waterpower, and Early Steam

    Open the coal measures¶

    Find and work a coal seam until you have a steady supply flowing to your works.

    Coal changes scale: it's why steam becomes practical on sites that outgrow woodland fuel.

    Do this: - retrieve Mine bituminous coal from a seam and deliver it to your works.
    yields: bituminous coal×50

    Rewards: bituminous coal×30

    Background

    Reality check¶

    Mines flood. That problem is why early steam engines get built at all.

    Win-path

    Make Coke for Industrial Heat¶

    Unlocks after: Open the Coal Measures

    Make coke for industrial heat¶

    Convert coal into coke: hotter, cleaner-burning, and suited for ironworks-scale firing.

    This is the fuel bridge between "some coal" and "serious heat on demand".

    Tip · Don't skip the coal buffer. Producing 40 coke consumes roughly 80 coal, and your blast furnace (built in the Steel chapter, but usable now) will need even more. The 80-coal stock task below is not optional — treat it as the minimum working supply.

    Do this: - retrieve Have or place a coke oven.
    yields: coke oven (beehive)×1 - produce Run a first coke burn and produce a starter batch. - retrieve Keep 80 coal on hand — this supply will be consumed making the coke you already burned plus the next batch.
    yields: bituminous coal×80

    Rewards: coke fuel×20

    Background

    About the oven form¶

    Early coke can be made in clamps/heaps; beehive ovens are a later high-throughput form. Functionally, the point is the same: controlled off-gassing to turn coal into coke.

    Coal ratio reality¶

    The coal->coke conversion is roughly 2:1 by mass in a beehive oven. 40 coke costs you ~80 coal. When the blast furnace is running it will consume coke continuously — keep your coal supply well ahead of your coke demand.

    Win-path

    Low-Pressure Boiler House¶

    Unlocks after: Open the Coal Measures

    Low-pressure boiler house¶

    Assemble a conservative stationary boiler plant: shell, firebox, pipes, feedwater loop, condensation/cooling hardware, and basic safety.

    This is low-pressure practice: enough to run early engines, not a racing locomotive.

    Tip · The cast-iron firebox and cylinder both require pig iron. Pig iron needs a small blast furnace (refractory bricks + coke + limestone -> blast_furnace_small). Build that first — hover blast_furnace_small and use Recipes (R by default) for the recipe.

    Tip · Limestone gotcha: mining limestone nodes yields raw_ore_limestone (the geologic ore form). The blast furnace recipe expects limestone_rock (discrete nodules). Convert at hand with raw_ore_limestone -> limestone_rock (1:1, free). Same trick applies to most "raw_ore_X" + "X_rock" pairs.

    Tip · Steam is a physical medium now, not a pretend token. Inspect pipe runs: you should see medium, pressure, temperature, source count, sink count, pipe material, and flow/limit. If the Inspector says no receiving machine, over-pressure, temperature, or corrosion, it is not being dramatic — it is saying the pipe network is wrong for that medium.

    Do this: - retrieve Have the boiler shell + cast-iron firebox ready. (Firebox needs pig iron — build a blast_furnace_small first if you haven't.)
    yields: riveted boiler shell×1 cast-iron firebox×1 - retrieve Have piping + feedwater hardware ready for a complete loop.
    yields: wrought-iron steam pipe×8 simple feedwater pump×1 wooden cooling tank×1 - retrieve Have basic safety + level indication online.
    yields: weighted safety valve×1 water level×1

    Rewards: wrought-iron steam pipe×4

    Background

    Non-negotiables¶

    • you must control pressure (relief)
    • you must control water level (don't expose iron to direct fire dry)
    • you must move water back in (feedwater loop)

    Pig iron in the Steam era¶

    Early boiler makers used small cupola-style furnaces long before the large Bessemer converters of the Steel age. Your blast_furnace_small recipe reflects this — it is buildable now, not gated behind the Steel chapter. Build it, charge it with coke + hematite + limestone, and tap enough pig iron to cast the firebox and cylinder.

    Why this gate exists¶

    Safe pressure vessels require repeatable metallurgy, tight machining tolerances, and standardized fasteners. If you can't make consistent plates, rivets, and seals, you don't get steam.

    Win-path

    Atmospheric Pump for a Wet Shaft¶

    Unlocks after: Low-Pressure Boiler House

    Atmospheric pump for a wet shaft¶

    Mines flood. When muscles and buckets can't keep up, you need something stronger.

    Build a simple pumping engine set. It uses the vacuum of condensing steam to lift a heavy beam, pulling water out of the deep earth. It's not about high pressure yet — it's about solving a problem with the best process you have right now.

    Do this: - retrieve Have the basic engine + cylinder assembly ready. (Cast-iron cylinder needs pig iron from your blast furnace.)
    yields: simple beam engine×1 steam cylinder (cast iron)×1 steam piston and rod×1 - retrieve Have condensation + sealing materials ready (cycle reliability).
    yields: jet condenser×1 steam gland packing×4

    Rewards: steam gland packing×4

    Background

    What makes it "early steam"¶

    Forget high pressure for now. Your goal here isn't to build a locomotive. Your goal is to keep a massive hole in the ground from filling with water. You cycle steam and condensation to pull a heavy beam up and let it drop. It is brute-force engineering, and it works perfectly.

    Win-path

    Turn Steam into Rotation¶

    Unlocks after: Atmospheric Pump for a Wet Shaft

    Turn steam into rotation¶

    Pumping water is great, but a factory needs to spin.

    Upgrade from "pumps only" to general drive. You'll need a flywheel to smooth the power, a crank to turn linear motion into rotation, and a governor so the whole thing doesn't tear itself apart under load.

    Once you have this, steam can drive the same shop logic as your waterwheels: shafts, belts, and stations. Except steam doesn't care if there's a drought.

    Do this: - retrieve Have the rotary power train parts ready.
    yields: flywheel×1 forged iron crank×1 flyball governor×1 - retrieve Have belts/pulleys/shafting ready to distribute steam power into a workshop.
    yields: cast-iron belt pulley×2 leather flat belt×4 wrought-iron line shaft×2

    Rewards: leather flat belt×4 cast-iron belt pulley×2

    Background

    Physics vs Engineering¶

    A piston pushing back and forth is physics. Turning that jerky motion into smooth, continuous power that can run a delicate lathe without snapping the drive belt? That's engineering.

    The flywheel eats the spikes in power. The governor feels the engine speeding up and automatically chokes the steam valve to slow it back down. That is your first taste of true automation.

    Win-path

    Mechanised Stamp Milling¶

    Unlocks after: Line-Shaft Workshop Bay, Turn Steam into Rotation

    Mechanised stamp milling¶

    Connect your powertrain to an ore crushing station. Cams lift and drop stamps to break raw ore into fine feed for furnaces.

    Route hematite from your ore supply into the mill input and connect the mill to live shaft power from your waterwheel or steam engine. The older hand-trip recipe still exists for emergencies; this quest is asking for the powered version because we are not pretending your Roblox arms are a 30-horsepower mill.

    This is throughput — not glamour.

    Tip · If the mill has ore but does not run: inspect it. A healthy setup should show live shaft/electric drive and a clear output. If transport is involved, inspect the chute/conveyor/pipe too: No source output available, No accepting destination, No drive power, and No destination is primed are all fixable setup problems, not random machine failures.

    Do this: - retrieve Have or place a stamp mill assembly connected to your powertrain.
    yields: stamp mill (manual trip hammer)×1 - retrieve Feed raw hematite ore to the stamp mill and connect live shaft power.
    yields: raw hematite ore×60 wrought-iron line shaft×1 - produce Produce crushed ore output while the stamp mill is connected to live shaft power.

    Rewards: crushed hematite×20 bituminous coal×20

    Background

    Why this sits here¶

    Your ironwork stops being "small batch heroic" and becomes "repeatable processing".

    That is the real steam-era shift.

    Checkpoint

    Tier 2 — Steam Age Online¶

    Unlocks after: Mechanised Stamp Milling

    Tier 2 complete — Steam Age online¶

    You have a real early-industrial works package:

    • waterwheel + line-shaft workshop distribution
    • coal access as a sustained fuel supply
    • coke production as an industrial heat chain
    • a conservative boiler house + steam loop discipline
    • steam justified first by mine drainage, then extended into rotary shop power
    • mechanised ore crushing throughput (stamp milling)

    Next: Steel takes this into high steam and DC power.

    Rewards: Engineer's Kit×1

    Background

    The line this draws¶

    From here forward, the game can assume: - prime movers exist (water + steam) - fuel is industrial (coal + coke chain) - power distribution exists (shafts/belts now; wires later) - ore prep is mechanised

    That's the on-ramp to high-pressure steam and electrical systems.
